太虚即气 EMERGING IN THE VOID

A contemporary experimental painting exhibition that explores the manifest of the existence within an intercultural context

Artist Statement

In an era when the subject is constantly expressed yet increasingly distorted, can the self still exist in a non-narrative, non-figurative way, one that exceeds the body, identity, and classificatory frameworks?

太虚即气 (EMERGING IN THE VOID) poster

My self-portrait series do not seek narrative or representation, but stages the emergence of an existence that is irreducible to classification, description, yet impossible to ignore.

I first place myself in front of a pure black background which is a model of “the void”. Zhang Zai (张载, a famous Chinese philosopher of Northern Song dynasty) pointed out that “the void is qi (太虚即气)”, that the void is not “emptiness,” but a field of tension that compels emergence and manifestation. It does not pre-set meaning, but it requires existence to respond in some way.

太虚即气 (The Void Is Qi), acrylic, ink, oil pastel on canvas, 2026. Appropriation of Chen Rong (陈容,1235-1266).

What traces are left behind, and what kind of qi emerges from then, depends on how I respond to the void, and how I exist at that moment. The emergence of the self is a phenomenological process. It is like a beam of light cast upon me, producing a shadow and a negative space on the wall. The shape of this negative space reflects my posture at that moment. The “light” is the tensive qi steamed from the void; My “posture” is my state of existence and the way I respond to the void, it is shi (势). The “negative space” is the form of qi takes after passing through me; and the “shadow” is the container of this negative space, which is the image. It is the trace of the process through which qi and I grind against each other.

太虚即气 (EMERGING IN THE VOID) scene. The self-portrait series, 2025-2026.

The pink stripe marks the “position” of the minimum condition of my presence. It is a fissure through which qi is received. This position is not only mine, but also of viewers. It guides them to occupy the same position as me. As a result, the image is no longer an act of depiction, but an event, a phenomenon, an encounter with qi. This is similar to the figures seen from behind in Caspar David Friedrich’s Wanderer above the Sea of Fog, or the figures described by 郭熙(Guo Xi) in 早春图 (Early Spring). These figures are neither Friedrich nor Guo Xi, yet they mark the presence and position of Friedrich, Guo Xi, and viewers. The image thus ceases to be a depiction of landscape and becomes an event, an encounter with the qi and sublime, and an articulation of how that encounter takes place.

David Friedrich, Wanderer above the Sea of Fog, 1818.

郭熙(Guo Xi), 早春图 (Early Spring), 1072.

郭熙(Guo Xi), 早春图 (Early Spring) detail, 1072.

I cannot detach myself from the external world (the figurative elements) in order to emerge independently and self-sufficiently. Chinese painting does not have a tradition of self-portraiture. Instead, literati painting employs a literary form of ‘metaphor’, allowing the subject to manifest through the external world. It is not a simple simulation of image, or a symbolization, but a correspondence of shi and qi. They do not narrate “who I am”; they indicate “how I exist” like an abstract painting does.

The appearance of limbs and faces already indicates that the “I” exist beyond the body; only then can the body appear as a “shadow”, “trace”, and a part of the "external world".

They therefore blur the boundary between form and figuration, abstraction and representation, the subject and the object.

The Warrior, acrylic and oil pastel on canvas, 2025.

Using the complete images of others directly as my self-portraits is my most extreme experiment, because an encounter and presence does not necessarily require new images. The pink stripe marks my position, transforming the qi into an encountered qi, carried qi. Through my presence, the image is transformed into an event, and viewers and I share a moment of encounter within the same field of tension.

Giant Magnolias on a Blue velvet Cloth, acrylic and oil pastel on canvas, 2025. Appropriation of Martin Johnson Heade (1819-1904).

(original artwork) Unknown Artist, Untitled, ink on silk, 1821?(now) Pink Chen, Untitled, acrylic on painting on silk, 2026.

《妙趣横生册》 (the Album of Endless Delight) is a practice of forcing the self into appearance through semi-controllable brushwork and ink. Through the continuous negotiation between rationality and “accidents”, it gradually manifests my presence.

《妙趣横生册》 (the Album of Endless Delight), ink on paper towel, 2026. Inspired by 徐渭 (Xu Wei, 1521-1593) and 八大山人 (Zhu Da, 1626-1705).

《虞美人》(Yu Mei Ren) , a poem by 李煜 (Li Yu, 937-978), ink on paper towel, 2026.

This collage-calligraphy work is composed exclusively of selected characters from my own writing, chosen from among multiple states of execution. Rather than presenting the self, the work exposes a condition in which the subject gains the ability to determine which states are allowed to appear and which are withheld. At this threshold, non-narrative expression becomes paradoxically narrative, not through language, symbolism, or storytelling, but through the act of selection itself. The work inhabits an unstable zone between narration and rupture, where meaning is neither fully constructed nor fully denied, and where visibility itself becomes the true site of tension.

In an era in which the self is excessively bodily and narrativized, 太虚即气 refuses to reproduce narrative and instead reduces the self to a state of existence that can be entered. It is not about who I am, but about how I emerge in the world, and how we exist together within it. What matters to me in this work as a whole is not the articulation of my Eastern identity or cultural belonging, but whether the work can continue to operate as a structure of existence after identity is stripped away.

Finally, my work serves as a self-justification of painting in the age of AI. Gerhard Richter’s theory of painting as the “processing” of images can no longer fully withstand the challenge posed by AI. My self-portrait series proposes an ontological understanding of figurative painting: painting is not the production or manipulation of images, but the temporal trace left by embodied practice on a material surface, an event of existence (Daisin) that cannot be outsourced to other, language, code, or instructions.

不系之舟 (Unmoored Boat), acrylic, ink, oil pastel on canvas, 2025.
